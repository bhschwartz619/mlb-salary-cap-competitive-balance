# Figure captions

Payroll in dollar terms of 2026 unless marked nominal. Regression tables report coefficients with standard errors in parentheses; * p < 0.10, ** p < 0.05, *** p < 0.01. Salary models (Table 3) use robust (HC1) standard errors; WAR-to-wins models (Table 2) use conventional standard errors.

**Figure 1** (fig01_payroll_over_time). Opening Day payroll by season, 2015–2026. Boxes show the middle 50% of teams, the line inside each box the median, and the orange line the league average ($125M in 2015, $174M in 2026). The shortened 2020 season is excluded. Data: Baseball-Reference; Cot's Baseball Contracts (Baseball Prospectus).

**Figure 2** (fig02_payroll_share_by_position). Share of Opening Day payroll by position, pooled over all team-seasons (2015–2026). Each player's salary is assigned to his primary position as listed by Cot's. Pitchers received 45% of payroll. Data: Baseball-Reference; Cot's Baseball Contracts (Baseball Prospectus).

**Figure 3** (fig03_wins_vs_war). Team wins against total team WAR, 2015–2026 (N = 330 team-seasons). Each additional WAR is associated with 0.98 more wins (95% CI 0.94–1.03); the intercept implies about 48 wins for a replacement-level team. Data: Baseball-Reference; Cot's Baseball Contracts (Baseball Prospectus).

**Figure 4** (fig04_war_value_by_position). Wins associated with one WAR at each position, from a regression of wins on WAR at all eleven positions (R² = 0.859, N = 330). Lines are 95% confidence intervals. Most positions are consistent with one win per WAR; relief pitchers sit above it. The model fits no better than total WAR alone (adjusted R² 0.854). Data: Baseball-Reference; Cot's Baseball Contracts (Baseball Prospectus).

**Figure 5** (fig05_correlation_matrix). Correlations among positional WAR, total WAR, and wins (N = 330 team-seasons). Total WAR correlates 0.92 with wins; positions are nearly independent of one another (no pair above 0.29). Data: Baseball-Reference; Cot's Baseball Contracts (Baseball Prospectus).

**Figure 6** (fig06_payroll_vs_war). Total team WAR against Opening Day payroll in 2026 dollars (N = 330). The line is the fitted regression with its 95% confidence band: about $14.4M per WAR (95% CI $11.5M–$19.3M). Payroll explains only 15% of the variation in WAR. Payroll is scaled to 2026 dollars by league-average payroll growth. Data: Baseball-Reference; Cot's Baseball Contracts (Baseball Prospectus).

**Figure 7** (fig07_payroll_war_wins_chain). Payroll is associated with wins only through WAR. Solid arrows show the two links (payroll → WAR, WAR → wins). The dashed arrow shows payroll's direct association with wins once WAR is controlled for, which is indistinguishable from zero; without WAR, the total association is 0.0683 wins per $1M. Robust (HC1) standard errors; *** p < 0.01. N = 330. Data: Baseball-Reference; Cot's Baseball Contracts (Baseball Prospectus).

**Figure 8** (fig08_war_per_10m). WAR associated with an additional $10M of payroll. Team: total WAR on total payroll. Hitters vs pitchers: total WAR on hitter and pitcher payroll together (pitchers 1.02, hitters 0.39; test of equal slopes p = 0.07). Position group: each group's WAR on that group's own payroll, attenuated by the mismatch between salary positions and where WAR is earned. Gray = 95% CI includes zero. Robust (HC1) standard errors. Data: Baseball-Reference; Cot's Baseball Contracts (Baseball Prospectus).

**Figure 9** (fig09_robustness). Robustness of the payroll–WAR estimate. Panel A: pooled estimate (0.69 WAR per $10M, dashed line) and with team or year fixed effects. Panel B: the same regression run on each season alone; single seasons are noisy (range 0.32–1.17), which supports pooling. Bars are 95% confidence intervals (robust HC1 standard errors). Data: Baseball-Reference; Cot's Baseball Contracts (Baseball Prospectus).

**Figure 10** (fig10_win_spread). How much of the spread in team wins payroll can explain. The actual standard deviation of wins is 12.7. Payroll (relative to the league average) explains 13% of the variance in wins, so removing payroll differences entirely would lower the spread only to about 11.9 wins. Pure luck over 162 games produces 6.4. Data: Baseball-Reference; Cot's Baseball Contracts (Baseball Prospectus).
