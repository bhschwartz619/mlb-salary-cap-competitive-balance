# Simulation figure captions

Main specification unless noted: MLB's 2027 proposal ($245.3M cap / $171.2M floor) restated as multiples of league-average CBT payroll and applied to each season; floor clubs buy only talent the cap releases; leftover talent goes to clubs between the thresholds in proportion to their room under the cap; $14.4M per WAR.

**Figure 11** (fig11_simulation_headline). Competitive balance under each payroll rule (main specification). Panel A: Noll-Scully ratio, the spread of winning percentage relative to pure luck; cap + floor lowers it from 2.16 to 2.04 (-5%). The dashed line is the actual 2019–2026 average (2.10); the simulated status quo sits slightly above it. Panel B: percentage-point gap in playoff rate between clubs in the top and bottom payroll quartiles; cap + floor narrows it from 38.9 to 20.1 points. Bars are at $14.4M per WAR; lines span results at $11M–$20M per WAR. Data: Baseball-Reference; Cot's Baseball Contracts (Baseball Prospectus). Simulation: 2,000 seasons per real season, 2019–2026.

**Figure 12** (fig12_playoff_rate_by_quartile). Simulated playoff rate by club payroll quartile under each rule (main specification, $14.4M per WAR). Quartiles are each club's status-quo CBT payroll rank within its season. Cap + floor raises the lowest quartile from 23% to 30% and lowers the highest from 62% to 50%; a floor alone leaves rates unchanged because, in the main specification, floor clubs can buy only talent a cap releases. Data: Baseball-Reference; Cot's Baseball Contracts (Baseball Prospectus). Simulation: 2,000 seasons per real season, 2019–2026.

**Figure 13** (fig13_clubs_bound_by_season). Clubs over the cap (up) and under the floor (down) each season, CBT payroll, MLB's 2027 proposal. Panel A restates the proposal as multiples of league-average payroll (cap 1.14x, floor 0.80x) and applies them to each season: 10–13 clubs over the cap and 8–12 under the floor every season. Panel B applies the fixed 2027 dollar amounts to past payrolls, which puts 19 clubs under the floor in the earliest seasons because payrolls were lower then, not because the rule binds harder. Data: Baseball-Reference; Cot's Baseball Contracts (Baseball Prospectus). Simulation: 2,000 seasons per real season, 2019–2026.

**Figure 14** (fig14_talent_movement_2024). Simulated change in each club's WAR under cap + floor, 2024 (main specification; CBT payroll in parentheses). Clubs over the $221.3M cap shed 38.0 WAR in total, led by the New York Mets (-8.7); clubs under the $154.5M floor gain what their mandated spending buys, and talent left over goes to clubs between the thresholds in proportion to their room under the cap. One WAR is worth about one win. Data: Baseball-Reference; Cot's Baseball Contracts (Baseball Prospectus). Simulation: 2,000 seasons per real season, 2019–2026.

**Figure 15** (fig15_playoff_gap_by_season). Playoff rate gap between top- and bottom-quartile payroll clubs, by season, under the status quo and cap + floor (main specification). The rule narrows the gap in every season, but by very different amounts: under cap + floor the gap ranges from -7.7 points in 2026 (low-payroll clubs ahead) to 38.6 in 2019, because each season starts from that year's real rosters and payrolls. Data: Baseball-Reference; Cot's Baseball Contracts (Baseball Prospectus). Simulation: 2,000 seasons per real season, 2019–2026.

**Figure 16** (fig16_simulation_robustness). Playoff rate gap under each rule across every specification: MLB's 2027 or 2028 proposal, thresholds scaled to each season or fixed in dollars, and floor clubs buying only talent the cap releases or competing for talent on the open market. Dots are at $14.4M per WAR; lines span $11M–$20M. Under scaled thresholds, a cap (with or without a floor) narrows the gap by about half in every specification, while a floor alone narrows it only when floor clubs can bid talent away from other clubs (open market). Fixed-dollar thresholds with an open-market floor produce the largest effects, because they put most clubs under the floor in early seasons. The dashed line is the status quo (39 points). Data: Baseball-Reference; Cot's Baseball Contracts (Baseball Prospectus). Simulation: 2,000 seasons per real season, 2019–2026.
